# Inkling 2 Mini: SGLang, vLLM and llama.cpp serving recipes

Research snapshot: 23 November 2026. **Mini**, not Inkling 2 Small: `thinkingmachines/Inkling-2-Mini@ed40e7da62`; 24 physical layers, 12 core layers visited three times (48 visits), 128 experts/top-8, official mixed NVFP4/MXFP8 expert checkpoint and a separate DSpark draft. Rates below are **output tokens/s**. Published rates are not a controlled leaderboard: prompts, quantization, speculative acceptance and context differ. In particular SGLang's quoted DSpark speed **simulates** 3.5 accepted tokens/step, not a measured end-to-end acceptance rate. [Model](https://huggingface.co/thinkingmachines/Inkling-2-Mini) · [SGLang architecture and benchmarks](https://www.sglang.io/blog/inkling-2-small-day0-support) · [vLLM recipe](https://recipes.vllm.ai/thinkingmachines/Inkling-2-Mini)

No published Mini recipe specifies FP8 KV dtype on the three launches above: SGLang documents an MXFP8 KV capability, but the [Spark config](https://github.com/sgl-project/sglang/blob/main/docs/src/snippets/configs/thinkingmachines/inkling-2-mini.jsx) sets no KV flag; vLLM's [recipe](https://recipes.vllm.ai/thinkingmachines/Inkling-2-Mini) likewise sets none. No published Mini-specific prefill chunk-size setting was found in the SGLang or vLLM launches. A [patched 2× RTX 3090 port](https://gist.github.com/quidow/a7ee52fa117da27c4dc25c37dafd27fd) uses `--max-num-batched-tokens 1024` and BF16 KV, but is **not** the official recipe. llama.cpp exposes `-b`/`-ub` and `--cache-type-k`/`--cache-type-v`; the [2× T4 example](https://github.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp/issues/32718) uses `-b 2048 -ub 64`. These are workload tuning knobs, not measured Mini wins on GB10. DSpark acceptance is workload-dependent: [vLLM PR #69028](https://github.com/vllm-project/vllm/pull/69028) reports a mean of 3.58 tokens/step on GB200 GSM8K, whereas the [GB10 hands-on](https://dev.classmethod.jp/en/articles/dgx-spark-inkling-2-mini-first-touch/) saw only 16% of proposed tokens accepted in one interval.

## llama.cpp next to TensorFold on TOP's GB10

`llama-cpp-job.sh` at `7fe6eaa70` ran the mainline llama.cpp recipe on TOP's GB10 (driver 580.173.02) through `swarm gpu-run`, TOP job `top-961eb7577d22`, with the same harness, prompts and settings as 188's `run-script.sh`: `tools/bench_concurrent.py`, 256 greedy tokens, 3 reps, the best cell per level. The release binaries run under the staged Arm GNU 14.3 glibc, because the job image's glibc predates 2.38; the host is unchanged. ## Ceiling and ordered missing work for cards 211, 210 and 243

The [188 GB10 receipt](https://github.com/plotarmordev/TensorFold/blob/54bf2c7f0cf3f72e5ab9ef9d4449c81a260fc6e0/docs/recipes/inkling-2-mini-cuda/README.md) counts **5.55 GB** of checkpoint weight reads per plain decoded token (core passes counted three times, eight of 128 experts each visit). At GB10's specified **273 GB/s**, plain single-stream is bounded by **49.2 tok/s**. Its measured **32.5** is 66% of that bound; even perfect plain kernels add at most **16.7 tok/s (+51%)**. These are **upper bounds, not promised gains**. Four streams reuse weight reads but pay more expert/KV traffic; do not multiply a one-stream ceiling by four as a prediction.

1. **CUDA DSpark verifier and device-side draft chain**, with depth priced by actual draft and verify costs: `zig/src/families/inkling2/mini_native.zig`, `mini_backend.zig` and CUDA drafter kernels. **211:** opens a path above the 49.2 plain ceiling; at 3.5 emitted tokens per target read the idealized bound is `3.5 × 49.2 = 172 tok/s` **before** extra expert rows, draft cost, acceptance losses and KV. A conservative empirical target is the [Strata GB10 74.3](https://github.com/Niko1221/Strata/releases/tag/v0.2.19) (+41.8 vs our 32.5, 2.29×), **not** a guarantee or a quant-matched comparison. **210:** already has Metal DSpark (`metal/dspark.zig`); do not file a duplicate. **243:** the laptop builds and serves the same CUDA engine at GB10's rate (32.2 against 32.5 tok/s), so this lever needs no separate Windows port; N1X WDDM bandwidth is not measured, so 273 GB/s and 49.2 tok/s are GB10 estimates, *not measured Windows ceilings*.
2. **Native FP4 expert tensor-core MMA + fused loop input/router kernels**: shared `zig/kernels/cuda/nvfp4_experts.cu` and `fp8_experts.cu` (via `zig/src/cuda/experts.zig`, `experts_mx.zig`), `zig/kernels/cuda/inkling2/ops.cu`, `mini_visit.zig`; Metal counterpart in `zig/kernels/metal/inkling2/experts.metal`, `metal/forward.zig`. **211/243:** reduce unpack and launch work to approach plain 49.2; **ceiling-bound incremental gain ≤16.7 tok/s (+51%)** on GB10 before drafting, shared with lever 3, not additive. The SGLang Mini [fusion result](https://www.sglang.io/blog/inkling-2-small-day0-support) is +22–24% on B300 at batch 1 and +30% on H200; neither transfers numerically to GB10. **210:** CUDA FP4 MMA itself N/A; fuse Metal controllers, measure against Mac plain and drafted controls, no asserted Metal gain.
3. **Remove remaining full-logit host read on Metal; benchmark decode graph/command-buffer replay**, `zig/src/families/inkling2/metal/backend.zig`, `metal/dspark.zig`, `metal/forward.zig`. **210:** on-device top-token and DSpark chain selection avoid the 200,058-logit host round trip. [Apple specifies 1.2 TB/s](https://support.apple.com/en-us/128107) for M5 Ultra; applying the GB10 receipt's 5.55 GB/token to it gives an **optimistic** 216 tok/s plain weight-read ceiling, at most +164 tok/s (+313%) above [PR #4465's 52.3 plain code-prompt tok/s](https://github.com/ashhart/TensorFold/pull/4465). This bound is **shared** with every plain Metal optimization, not a predicted host-read gain: Mac weight traffic and effective bandwidth have not been measured. **211:** already has device sampling and CUDA graphs; the missing GB10 counterpart is fused attention GEMV in `mini_visit.zig`/CUDA attention kernels, within the **same** remaining 16.7 tok/s plain gap. **243:** the laptop already runs CUDA device sampling and graphs (32.2 tok/s at one stream); its gain from this GEMV work is unmeasured until rerun on WDDM.

For any lever, use control/candidate/control medians (≥6 decode samples), identical context/prompts, and reference/token checks. Quantized GGUF trades quality: ggml-org IQ4_NL gives **95.5% same top token** against its BF16 ([GGUF card](https://huggingface.co/ggml-org/Inkling-2-Mini-GGUF)); it cannot silently replace the official checkpoint in a token-for-token gate. Do not treat SGLang's simulated 3.5 accepted tokens or a draft count of 7/8 as a measured gain on our prompts.
