# Drama 3 pruned NVFP4 checkpoints on main

Both published compact checkpoints, [`plotarmordev/drama-3-NVFP4-Pruned`](https://huggingface.co/plotarmordev/drama-3-NVFP4-Pruned)
(7,369 routed experts kept) and [`plotarmordev/drama-3-NVFP4-Pruned-5900`](https://huggingface.co/plotarmordev/drama-3-NVFP4-Pruned-5900)
(5,900 kept), served by `tensorfold-native` built from main, beside main's FP8 Drama 3 on the same machine.
[`tools/zig/drama3_checkpoints_check.py`](../../../tools/zig/drama3_checkpoints_check.py) makes every figure here.

## Method

Each checkpoint gets its own server: `serve CKPT --parallel 2 --voices DIR --default-voice VOICE --cfg-gamma 1.5 --cfg-batched`,
a reference-backed catalog voice so guidance acts. Every request carries `"seed": 7` and `"response_format": "pcm"`.

- **Experts held.** The count in the startup line `routed experts held N of 10240`: 10,240 for FP8, 7,369 and 5,900.
- **Repeat check.** A whole reply (text A) and a streamed reply (text B) are sent alone, then again; each PCM sha256 must
  equal the first.
- **Concurrent against solo.** The same two requests are then sent at once, on the server's two lanes; each must equal
  its solo PCM.
- **Load.** Seconds from starting the process to `/health` answering 200.
- **Peak memory.** `GET /memory` after the requests: `device.peak_bytes` on CUDA; on macOS, where the Drama host reports
  no device figure, `process.lifetime_peak_physical_footprint_bytes`.
- **First audio, total, RTF.** The first solo streamed reply, timed by the client: seconds to its first PCM chunk and
  to its end; RTF is total seconds over audio seconds (bytes / 88,200), so below 1 is faster than real time.

## Mac

Apple M5 Ultra, 256 GB, macOS 27.0; main `d1696b497`, `zig build native -Doptimize=ReleaseFast` with Zig 0.17.0
(binary sha256 `83db6000…0a5561e59`). The voice is `synthetic`: 5.5 s of Drama 3's built-in voice, generated by
`drama3-generate` and encoded with `drama3-voice`.
Raw: [`mac/results.json`](mac/results.json), [`mac/server-pruned.log`](mac/server-pruned.log),
[`mac/server-pruned-5900.log`](mac/server-pruned-5900.log).

The checkpoints are local copies of the published ones: each of their 10 LFS files has the Hugging Face repository's
sha256. Main has no FP8 checkpoint on this Mac, so there is no FP8 row.

| Checkpoint | Experts held | Load (s) | Peak footprint (GiB) | First audio (s) | Total (s) | Audio (s) | RTF |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| pruned | 7369 | 8.9 | 10.21 | 0.94 | 3.26 | 4.46 | 0.730 |
| pruned-5900 | 5900 | 8.5 | 10.18 | 0.94 | 3.38 | 4.55 | 0.742 |

Verdict: pass. Both held their experts; whole and streamed replies equalled their repeats and their concurrent lanes.

- **Memory.** The footprint leaves out the packed experts, which Metal reads in place from the mapped files: 13.04 GB
  (7,369) and 10.44 GB (5,900), beside 6.36 GB of BF16 weights. The startup line keeps 28.5 GiB wired between requests
  for the 7,369-expert checkpoint, inside the load limit of 70% of RAM.
- **Second lane.** The concurrent streamed reply recorded first audio at 6.07 s (7,369 experts) and 6.22 s (5,900),
  with total times of 8.47/8.66 s and RTF 1.90 for both: its lane built its own reference prefill while the other spoke.
- **Timing limitation.** This Mac run predates the checker's `read1()` correction. Its first-audio figures time a
  buffered 65,536-byte read, so they are upper bounds on first-byte latency. PCM hashes, total times and RTF are
  unaffected; a new Mac run is needed for first-byte latency.

## Spark

DGX Spark (GB10, sm_121, 128 GB unified memory), driver 580.173.02, CUDA 13.0; this branch at `132bd1238`, GPU job
`bottom-49eafd253777` on 12 Nov 2026. The job image has no C++ compiler for nvcc, so
[`prebuild-fatbins.sh`](prebuild-fatbins.sh) first built the commit's kernel images on the Spark through `bottom shell`,
from a clean checkout of `SHA`: `PILOT=~/Documents/drama3-pilot-20261014 IMAGE=<job image>
docs/receipts/drama3-pruned-main/prebuild-fatbins.sh` (`zig build fatbins -Dsm=121` in the job image with g++ added,
into `tensorfold-fatbins-SHA` in the pilot folder with `SHA256SUMS`; sha256 of those sums `fc8e7105…`).
[`run-script.sh`](run-script.sh) takes no arguments, so the GPU door runs it as is:
`job start gpu bottom plotarmordev/TensorFold SHA docs/receipts/drama3-pruned-main/run-script.sh`. It checks the
kernel images, builds the server with `-Dgpu=spark -Dfatbins` (no nvcc), makes a synthetic voice the way the Mac's was
made, and runs the check over the FP8 checkpoint and both pruned ones from the pilot folder. It pauses no service.
Raw: [`spark/results.json`](spark/results.json) and the three `spark/server-*.log`.

Inputs (sha256 over each checkpoint's safetensors and `codec.pth`): FP8 `03a65274…`, pruned `abd1d1c0…`,
pruned-5900 `ac30e4e1…`.

| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| fp8 | 10240 | 27.6 | 39.57 | 6.07 | 11.83 | 5.02 | 2.359 |

| pruned-5900 | 5900 | 26.0 | 19.29 | 5.65 | 10.89 | 4.88 | 2.233 |

Verdict: pass. Each held its experts (pruned ones: NVFP4 in 40 of 40 layers, compact routers in 36 of 40); whole and
streamed replies equalled their repeats and their concurrent lanes. First audio is timed at the first bytes (`read1()`).

- **Against FP8.** The pruned checkpoints take 45% and 51% less device memory, load 3.1 and 1.6 s faster, and
  reach first audio about 0.4 s sooner, at a similar RTF.
- **Slower than real time.** With guidance and a reference voice, every checkpoint runs at RTF 2.2-2.4 on the Spark.
  Unguided, the FP8 voice generation in the same job ran at RTF 1.43.
- **Second lane.** At once, the two lanes take turns: the concurrent streamed reply reached first audio at 11.1-11.9 s
  and ended at 20.3-22.0 s (RTF 4.0-4.4), the whole reply at 21.5-23.0 s.
